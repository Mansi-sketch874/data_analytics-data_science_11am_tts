# LAN, MAN, and WAN

LAN, MAN, and WAN are types of computer networks classified mainly by the geographical area they cover.

## 1. LAN: Local Area Network

LAN stands for **Local Area Network**. It connects computers and devices within a small geographical area, such as a home, classroom, office, building, or laboratory.

### Examples of LAN

- A home Wi-Fi network connecting phones, laptops, and a printer.
- A school computer laboratory.
- Computers connected inside an office building.

### Features of LAN

- Covers a small area.
- Usually owned and managed by one person or organization.
- Provides high data-transfer speed.
- Has comparatively low setup and maintenance costs.
- Commonly uses Ethernet cables or Wi-Fi.

### Common LAN Devices

- switch;
- router;
- network interface card;
- Ethernet cable;
- wireless access point.

## 2. MAN: Metropolitan Area Network

MAN stands for **Metropolitan Area Network**. It connects multiple LANs across a city, large campus, or metropolitan area. It is larger than a LAN but smaller than a WAN.

### Examples of MAN

- A network connecting the branches of a bank across one city.
- A university network connecting buildings across a large campus.
- A city-wide cable or public-service network.

### Features of MAN

- Covers a town, city, or large campus.
- Connects several LANs.
- May be managed by a large organization, telecommunications provider, or public authority.
- Usually has higher cost and more complex administration than a LAN.
- Can use fiber-optic cables, wireless links, or leased communication lines.

## 3. WAN: Wide Area Network

WAN stands for **Wide Area Network**. It connects networks over a large geographical area, such as different cities, countries, or continents.

### Examples of WAN

- The Internet.
- A multinational company network connecting offices in different countries.
- A bank network connecting branches across a nation.

### Features of WAN

- Covers a very large area.
- Connects LANs and MANs.
- Often uses leased lines, fiber-optic cables, satellites, cellular networks, or the public Internet.
- Usually has higher setup and operating costs.
- Requires strong security, monitoring, and professional management.
- May have lower speed or higher delay than a LAN because data travels farther.

## Difference Between LAN, MAN, and WAN

| Feature | LAN | MAN | WAN |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full form | Local Area Network | Metropolitan Area Network | Wide Area Network |
| Coverage | Room, building, or small site | City or large campus | Country or worldwide |
| Main purpose | Connect nearby devices | Connect networks across a city | Connect networks across large distances |
| Speed | Usually high | Usually medium to high | Varies and may be lower than LAN |
| Cost | Low | Medium to high | High |
| Ownership | Individual or organization | Organization or service provider | Multiple providers or organizations |
| Example | Office network | City bank network | Internet |

## Advantages

### Advantages of LAN

- Fast sharing of files, printers, and applications.
- Easy communication between nearby users.
- Lower cost compared with larger networks.
- Centralized data and device management.

### Advantages of MAN

- Connects offices or buildings within a city.
- Allows resources to be shared across multiple LANs.
- Supports communication between branches in the same metropolitan area.

### Advantages of WAN

- Connects employees and systems across distant locations.
- Supports global communication and remote work.
- Allows organizations to share data and applications internationally.

## Disadvantages

- A LAN failure can affect users in the local area.
- A MAN is more expensive and complex than a LAN.
- A WAN is expensive to operate and requires stronger security.
- WAN communication can experience delay, outages, and security risks.

## Role in Data Analytics

Networks allow analysts and organizations to collect, transfer, and access data. A LAN can connect analysts to local databases and shared files. A MAN can connect data systems across branches in one city. A WAN can transfer data between offices, cloud services, data centers, and international teams.

## In One Sentence

LAN connects devices in a small area, MAN connects networks across a city, and WAN connects networks across countries or the world.
